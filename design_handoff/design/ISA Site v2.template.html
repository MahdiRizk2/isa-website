What a semester at ISA looks like

Everything is open to everyone. No prior knowledge, membership, or dues needed. Rooms and times are posted in the Updates chat each semester.

Every Thursday

Dua Kumayl & Discussion

Our most loved and consistent program. We recite Dua Kumayl together, then move into a student-led discussion on a topic chosen by members. Topics range across faith, practice, history, and campus life.

Discussion resourcesRequest a topic
Every other Tuesday

Book Club & Dua Tawassul

We read a book together over the semester and talk it through a section at a time, then close with Dua Tawassul. Members pick the next book.

At least one sisterhood and one brotherhood event a month: game nights, sports, picnics, watch parties, hikes, and outings. The easiest way to get to know people outside the program room.

About

The Shia Muslim student community at Michigan

Student-run since its founding, ISA is a member of the Muslim Coalition at the University of Michigan and open to anyone who wants to learn.

Mission

The mission of the Islamic Society of Ahlulbayt at the University of Michigan is to provide a welcoming space for Muslims and other interested individuals to learn, connect, and serve in accordance with the teachings of the Quran, the Prophet (SAW), and the Ahlulbayt (AS).

ISA is established for the express purpose of providing an atmosphere of reflection and contemplation for interested individuals, in accordance with the sacred teachings of Islam. We are a core member of our Muslim community at U-M and firmly stand against Islamophobic and anti-Shi'i sentiment.

We do this through raising awareness, discussions, social events, fundraisers, and speaker events.

Student-run

ISA is led by a student executive board elected each year. Programs are planned and run by students.

Part of the Muslim Coalition

ISA organizes under the Muslim Coalition at U-M alongside other Muslim student organizations, and collaborates with them through the year.

Open to all

Shia, Sunni, Muslim or not, student or not. If you want to learn or just be around good people, you are welcome.

Your support keeps Thursday nights running.

ISA is funded mostly through university grants applied for event by event. That funding is irregular, and it is often the only thing holding programs back. Gifts from alumni, families, and community members fill the gap.
